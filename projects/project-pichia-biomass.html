Effect of Glucose Concentration on the Biomass Yield of Pichia pastoris

GS115 strain · OD600 time series · One-way ANOVA

In this project, I investigated how varying glucose concentrations (2%, 10%, 20%, 30%) affect the biomass yield of the GS115 strain of Pichia pastoris grown in YPD medium. Biomass was tracked over 24 hours by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD600) at multiple time points.:contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

I prepared four flasks with identical YPD broth but different glucose levels, inoculated them with the same volume of GS115, and incubated them at 27 °C and 120 rpm in a shaking incubator. At each time point, samples were collected, frozen, resuspended in water, and read on a spectrophotometer, with duplicate readings used to compute means and standard deviations.

I interpreted the results as an osmotic and metabolic stress effect. Overall, this project strengthened my understanding of predictive microbiology and bioprocess optimization, how carbon supply, growth kinetics, and statistical analysis come together for biomass or protein production.

Project details

  • Organism: Pichia pastoris GS115 (Invitrogen)
  • Medium: YPD with 2%, 10%, 20%, and 30% glucose
  • Measurements: OD600 at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, and 24 hours (duplicate readings)
  • Methods: spectrophotometry, growth curves, summary table, one-way ANOVA on 24 h OD600
  • Key finding: biomass increases up to ~20% glucose, then declines at 30% due to osmotic stress and metabolic limits
